Heart and lipids 11

  • Heart and lipids

    Total cholesterol

    The overall amount of cholesterol carried in your blood.

    What it is

    Cholesterol is a waxy fat your body uses to build cells and make hormones. Total cholesterol adds together the cholesterol carried by all the lipoproteins in your blood.

    Why we test it

    It is the starting point of a lipid profile. On its own it says little, so we read it alongside HDL, LDL and the ratios in this group.

    Included in

    • Foundation PanelBaseline and Optimise membershipsAt-home test kit
    • Comprehensive PanelPerformance membershipAt-home phlebotomy

    Every result is interpreted by a clinician alongside your other markers and your history. No single marker is read on its own.

  • Heart and lipids

    HDL cholesterol

    High-density lipoprotein cholesterol

    Cholesterol carried by HDL, which moves cholesterol back to the liver.

    What it is

    HDL particles collect cholesterol from the tissues and carry it back to the liver to be processed. This marker measures how much cholesterol HDL is carrying.

    Why we test it

    HDL is part of the balance of your lipid profile and is used to work out the ratios we report.

    Included in

    • Foundation PanelBaseline and Optimise membershipsAt-home test kit
    • Comprehensive PanelPerformance membershipAt-home phlebotomy

    Every result is interpreted by a clinician alongside your other markers and your history. No single marker is read on its own.

    HbA1c

    Glycated haemoglobin

    Your average blood sugar over roughly the last two to three months.

    What it is

    Sugar in the blood attaches to haemoglobin in your red blood cells. Red cells last around three months, so the share that is glycated reflects your average blood sugar over that time.

    Why we test it

    It smooths out day-to-day swings, which makes it one of the clearest markers to track between tests.

    What it is

    Ferritin stores iron inside your cells, and a small amount circulates in the blood in proportion to those stores.

    It is one of the most useful iron markers, particularly if you train regularly. As it also rises with inflammation, we read it alongside hs-CRP.
